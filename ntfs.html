Write to an NTFS drive on your Mac

Your Mac can open and read drives formatted in Windows' NTFS format, but it can't write to them. There are two ways to fix that.

Free: reformat it as exFAT

exFAT works on Mac and Windows, with no file-size limit. Reformatting erases the drive, so copy your files somewhere else first.

  1. Open Disk Utility (in Applications, then Utilities).
  2. Choose View, then Show All Devices.
  3. In the sidebar, select the drive itself (the top line), not the volume under it.
  4. Click Erase. Choose Format: ExFAT and Scheme: GUID Partition Map.
  5. Click Erase, then Done. Copy your files back.

Best when you just need one drive that works everywhere.

Keep NTFS: install an NTFS driver

A driver adds NTFS writing to macOS, so Finder can copy, rename and delete files on the drive without erasing anything. Best when the drive has to stay NTFS, for example a Windows backup drive or one shared with Windows PCs at work.

  • Paragon NTFS for Mac: paid, with a free trial.
  • Microsoft NTFS for Mac by Tuxera: paid, with a free trial.

Both are made by long-standing storage companies. DriveVitals isn't connected to either of them.
